It's the first question most schools ask us, and it's a fair one. No two schools run the same way. One has boarding houses and lessons on Saturday mornings. Another calls its pupils students, its sanctions behaviour points, and its Year 9 "Remove".

Software that only works one way asks the school to change. EarlySight works the other way round: the school sets it up to match how it already runs.

## Your data, from your MIS

EarlySight reads the attendance, behaviour and grades your staff already record. It works from your MIS's own exports, or can integrate directly via Wonde (subject to MIS API availability). There's nothing new for staff to record. See [which MIS it works with](/works-with-your-mis/).

## Your words

Every school has its own vocabulary, and a dashboard in someone else's words gets misread.

- **Your names for things.** Pupils or students, forms or tutor groups, sanctions or behaviour points, house points or merits, form tutors, year leaders, detentions, periods. Set them once in Admin and every page uses them.
- **Your year groups.** If your Year 9 is called "Remove", EarlySight asks once which year it is, and remembers.
- **Your codes.** Attendance codes, behaviour types and grade names arrive as your MIS records them. Anything EarlySight doesn't recognise is asked about once, then remembered for every upload after.

## Your week and your grades

- **Your timetable.** Lessons on Saturdays, and more than five periods a day, are handled as they are.
- **Your grades.** Each year group and type of grade has its own scale: 9 to 1, letters, or words like "Secure" and "Exceeding". A subject that grades differently can have its own.
- **Your subjects.** "Eng Lit", "English Literature" and "ENG LIT (9-1)" are one subject. You link the spellings once, so a pupil's line runs unbroken from KS2 to A level.
- **Your weightings.** Each behaviour type counts for what the school says. A detention can weigh more than a code, and a Headteacher's Award can count as ten house points.

## Your groups

This is where schools differ most, and where the questions get interesting.

Free school meals, SEND and Pupil Premium come as standard, split the way the DfE splits them. But the groups that matter most to a school are often its own: boarding houses, a literacy intervention, a mentoring scheme, a sports team, a club, or the primary school a pupil came from.

In EarlySight, a school can set up as many of these as it likes.

- **Two kinds of group.** Some groups hold each pupil once, like houses. Others let a pupil be in several, like clubs. EarlySight works out which from the memberships.
- **Membership is dated.** A pupil who joined an intervention in January, or moved house in September, counts in the group from that date. Figures at each report count the pupils who were in the group at that point, so a comparison before and after isn't muddled by who joined later.
- **Easy to add.** Upload a list, paste a set of pupil numbers, or pick pupils (or a whole form) on screen.
- **Then they're everywhere.** Each group can be compared with everyone else on the subject, class and year pages, used as a filter, and shown beside a pupil's name on their page.

**Two safeguards come built in.**

- **A new group is visible to senior leaders only** until an admin opens it to all staff, so a sensitive list is never shown widely by default.
- **Small groups never show a figure.** Below the school's minimum size (five, unless you change it), a group shows "fewer than 5". One pupil can't be picked out of a group of two.

## Your triggers

Every flag on every list comes from a rule the school sets, not one we've chosen for you. A few examples:

- **Attendance.** The line below which attendance counts as low, your attendance target, and how far below the rest of the year a form has to be before it stands out.
- **Behaviour.** How much a pupil's last ten school days must rise above their usual before it's a sudden rise, and how far above their longer record before it's a long-term one. A minimum number of incidents comes first, so one bad day doesn't raise an alarm.
- **Missed lessons.** How many missed lessons, and how much more often than the pupil's other lessons, before [subject avoidance](/lesson-attendance/) is flagged.
- **Grades and effort.** How far a pupil's position in a subject has to fall, and how far effort has to drop, before they appear.
- **New joiners.** How many school days a pupil is on roll before any concern is raised, so a new arrival has time to settle.

The good news follows the school's rules too: what counts as excellent attendance, and which milestones to celebrate. "Going well" sits beside "Needs attention", with the same number of pupils on each.

Every flag comes with its reason in plain numbers, so staff can see which rule a pupil met and why.

## Your people

Each member of staff sees their own pupils: form tutors their form, year leaders their year, the head of sixth form Years 12 and 13, and senior leaders the whole school. The limits are enforced on the server, not just hidden on the screen.

## So, will it work with your school?

If your staff take registers and record behaviour and grades in your MIS, almost certainly. The setup is about your school's choices, not a list of changes for you to make.
