No PE kit. No calculator. Wrong shoes. Homework not handed in. In most schools these go into the behaviour log as sanctions, alongside disruption and defiance.

For some pupils that's fair: they forgot. For others, the missing item is a bill their family couldn't pay. The behaviour log can't tell the difference. Your data can, if you ask it the right question.

## What it costs

The DfE's own survey of parents put the cost of uniform and PE kit for a secondary pupil at £442 in 2023/24. In a Children's Society poll of about 3,000 parents last December, two in five said they struggle to afford the uniform and PE kit their school requires. The same poll found children being given detentions, put in isolation and even excluded over uniform their families couldn't afford.

That's a lot of families. In January 2026, 26.5% of pupils in England were eligible for free school meals: more than 2.2 million children.

## What shows up in the data

Pupils eligible for free school meals are suspended far more often. In 2024/25 there were 26.7 suspensions for every 100 of these pupils, against 5.4 for everyone else: nearly five times as many.

Suspensions are the serious end, but the pattern starts much lower down. The Education Policy Institute surveyed over 5,000 secondary teachers this year. Schools with more disadvantaged pupils were more likely to list uniform as a reason for removing a pupil from a lesson.

None of this proves that cost is the cause. But it does mean a school should check, rather than assume that every "no kit" code is about behaviour.

## De-sensitisation to sanctions

In sample data that we reviewed for a large school, there were several children who had virtually flawless behaviour until all of a sudden they started picking up sanctions in December and January. The school's instinct was to see them as pupils who needed substantial support and escalation. Our Pupil View on the platform gave a much better insight. Their first codes were all landing in PE and Maths and virtually all of them were "Equipment Codes". Additionally, these were FSM pupils. So what had happened? The deterioration in behaviour was because they had lost their compasses, protractors and PE tops. Either their parents couldn't afford replacements OR the pupil didn't want to ask. 

However what happened next was even more interesting. Pupils don't distinguish between their misdemeanours - to them a "Code" for a missing pencil case is fundamentally identical to a Code for skipping a lesson. By sanctioning pupils for not having a pencil case, the school was making other sanctions less impactful. Think about how a pupil feels about their first code. If it was outside of their control then why would they care about receiving a second? Across this cohort we saw increases in other sanctions about two months after the Equipment codes began. Our platform could have helped to identify the source of the problem before it escalated.


## Two changes this year

From this September, secondary schools in England can require no more than three branded uniform items, plus a tie. Free school meals have also been extended to every family on Universal Credit.

That second change matters for your data. Your free school meals group will be larger this year than last, so a change in its figures may simply be a change in who is in it.
